## Verdict

**Use `La = 5`.** It is the fastest of the three *and* the best storage layout.
The two axes do not trade off against each other — the brief's premise that they
did rests on an inverted mapping (below).

## The premise correction (read this first)

The brief mapped chunk sizes to the wrong `La` for 5 and 7. A production chunk is
one level-`La` ancestor column of `7^(12-La)` L12 cells, so:

| La | chunk cells | chunk (f32, raw) | global chunk slots = `ncells(IGeo7, La)` | within the 65 536 cache cap? |
|---:|------------:|-----------------:|-----------------------------------------:|:--|
| 5 | `7^7` = 823 543 | **3.14 MiB** | 168 072 | no |
| 6 | `7^6` = 117 649 | 0.45 MiB | 1 176 492 | no |
| 7 | `7^5` = 16 807 | 0.06 MiB | 8 235 432 | yes |

Measured directly (`DGG.ncells(IGeo7System(), La)`; global L12 = 138 412 872 012).

So it is `La = 7`, not `La = 5`, that fits under `_TILE_CELL_CACHE_MAX` /
`_CHUNK_CAP_CACHE_MAX` (`1 << 16`), and it is `La = 5`, not `La = 7`, that lands
in the ~1-10 MB community sweet spot with a manageable ~168 K global slots.
**Storage prefers the *smaller* `La`.** And so does compute.

-> `DGGSpace(chunklevel=La)`. `chunks` never passed. `budget = 2^30`.

## Why: the caches are not the mechanism

Per-cell allocation falls monotonically as `La` gets *coarser*, in every region,
and does not jump at the 65 536 cache boundary between La=7 (cached) and La=6
(not cached):

```
mid-lat   La=7 12.6   La=6 5.3   La=5 4.4   La=4 4.3   KiB/cell
polar     La=7 234.8  La=6 136.2 La=5 70.7             KiB/cell
```

If the `TileCells` / chunk-cap caches were the dominant term, crossing the cap
would cost, not save. What actually dominates is **per-chunk fixed overhead**
— block setup, restricted-tree construction, source-tile pairing, boundary-ring
geometry — amortised over the chunk's cells. Bigger chunks amortise it better.
Losing the caches at La=6 and La=5 is real but second-order; the caches are
themselves an allocation, and at La=7 they do not repay their own cost.

The floor is per-chunk *parallelism*, and it is what stops the trend at La=4:
the lazy plan threads over source chunks within one destination chunk, so at
mid-latitude (1-4 DEM tiles per subtree) a single-chunk destination starves.
La=4 has the *best* per-busy-core rate (36 400 cells/s/core vs 33 000 at La=5)
but the *worst* utilisation (1.49x vs 2.36x), so its wall regresses 31 %. At the
south pole, where a subtree meets 180-360 tiles and utilisation is 6.4-7.3x
across the board, La=5's advantage is pure work reduction with no parallelism
confound — and it is the largest there (3.07x over La=7).

## Memory: coarser `La` is nearly free

The 111 GiB figure at `cap:-89.9` La=5 is allocation *churn*, not footprint.
Peak RSS, one process per `La`, `/usr/bin/time -v`, the polar (worst) case,
`budget = 2^30`:

| La | peak RSS | alloc churn | churn / RSS |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 7 | 2.44 GiB | 45.6 GiB | 19x |
| 6 | 2.46 GiB | 61.5 GiB | 25x |
| 5 | **2.71 GiB** | 113.9 GiB | 42x |

La=5 costs **+0.27 GiB peak per worker** over La=7 — 11 %. The `budget` keyword,
not `La`, sets the working set, so the existing "~2.5 GiB/worker at
`budget=2^30`" sizing for the W x T layout survives the change unmodified.
La=5's higher GC pressure (13.8 s of 114.7 s, 12 %, vs 5.5 s of 45.2 s, 12 %) is
proportionally the same.

## Over-coverage: the cost of whole subtrees

A coarser `La` covers the target with fewer, larger complete subtrees, so it
computes more cells for the same region. Measured against the exact spherical
area of a lon/lat box at 45N:

| box side | ideal L12 cells | La=5 | La=6 | La=7 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 deg | 2 351 677 | 2.80x | 1.65x | 1.23x |
| 2 deg | 9 322 461 | 1.86x | 1.26x | 1.11x |
| 5 deg | 56 650 910 | 1.32x | 1.11x | 1.04x |
| 10 deg | 215 395 926 | 1.15x | 1.06x | 1.02x |
| 20 deg | 765 888 426 | 1.08x | 1.03x | 1.01x |

For a *small* target region this dominates: at `cap:-89.9`, La=7 finishes the
region in 42.7 s and La=5 takes 113.5 s, because La=5 computed 8.2x the cells.
**Regional one-off regrids of sub-degree areas should still use La=7.**

For the global land run it does not dominate. Over-coverage there is set by the
coastline, not by the region: chunk widths are ~55 km (La=5), ~21 km (La=6),
~8 km (La=7) against a ~1e6 km coastline and 1.49e8 km^2 of land, i.e. roughly
+18 % / +7 % / +3 %. And most of that surplus is **free**: the production source
is a `PartialGrid` over the ~26 450 real land tiles, so an over-covered
open-ocean cell has no source tile to pair with, gets pruned in the plan, and is
never clipped. Only coastal partial subtrees actually cost.

Headline number to carry forward: **La=5 sustains ~78 700 cells/s in one

~14 500 cells/s (~2 000 per core) in the south-polar tile pile-up. La=5 is
~1.7-1.9x better than La=7 end to end once over-coverage is charged against it.

## Surprises

1. **The brief's La<->chunk-size mapping was inverted for 5 and 7.** La=5 is the
   3.14 MiB / 168 K-slot layout the storage guidance actually wants; La=7 is the
   67 KiB / 8.2 M-slot layout that is operationally painful. Compute and storage
   agree, and both point at La=5. There is no trade to weigh.
2. **Crossing the 65 536-cell cache cap costs nothing measurable.** La=6 (cache
   off) beats La=7 (cache on) in every region, and allocates half as much per
   cell. The caches are sized for a regime this workload is not in.
3. **La=4 regresses 31 %** despite the best per-core rate — the loss is
   utilisation, not work. La=5 is a genuine interior optimum on wall time.
4. **`DGGSpace` build is 40x cheaper at La=5** (0.30 s vs 12.1 s), because
   `_chunkwindows` scans the whole global level-`La` ancestor set. This is a
   fixed cost per space per worker and is pure waste at La=7.
5. **Chunking is bitwise-neutral.** Same cell, same float, whichever `La`
   computed it — so `La` can be revisited later without invalidating output.
6. **Peak RSS is almost flat in `La`** (2.44 / 2.46 / 2.71 GiB) even though
   allocation churn more than doubles. `budget` governs the working set; `La`
   does not. The worker-sizing arithmetic does not need revisiting.
7. **The south pole is ~5x more expensive per cell than mid-latitude** at every
   `La` (14.5 K vs 78.7 K cells/s at La=5) and allocates 16x more per cell.
   Worker scheduling should treat polar chunk ranges as heavy, or the tail will
   set the wall clock.

## Caveats

- The mid-latitude regions use synthetic tiles (no real GLO-90 N45/E010 in the
  cache); the polar cap uses the real S89/S90 GeoTIFFs. Tile decode is excluded
  from the comparison by a shared warm LRU, so this affects neither the ranking
  nor the rates.
- Both mid-latitude boxes fall inside a single level-5 cell, so their La=5 rows
  are the same destination subtree; they agree to 0.3 %, which is a repeatability
  check rather than an independent second region. The polar cap is the
  independent one.
- Mid-latitude utilisation is 1.8-2.4x of 8 threads: a single `-t 8` process is
  parallelism-starved there. The per-busy-core column is the right basis for
  sizing the W x T layout; the single-process column is not.
- The machine was shared (load ~20) throughout. Warm run-to-run spread was under
  1 %, so contention did not move the ranking.

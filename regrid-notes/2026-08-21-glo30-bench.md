# Does the perf ladder carry to GLO-30 / L13? Yes — 2.96x, bit-identical

Companion data: `2026-08-21-glo30-bench.ndjson` (63 measurements, every number
below).

Validating `2026-08-20-perf-ladder.md` (2.75x on GLO-90 -> IGeo7 L12) at the
next resolution up. **It carries, and slightly exceeds itself.**

| | GLO-90 -> L12, La=5 | **GLO-30 -> L13, La=6** |
|---|---:|---:|
| speedup, `-t 1` | 2.75x | **2.96x** |
| mid-latitude column | 2.80x | **2.87x** |
| allocation per column | −43 % | **−36 %** |
| bit-identical | yes | **yes, all 4 columns** |

---

## 1. Setup

Two worktrees, both clean, same CR pin (`6a4b997`), differing only in library
code and the GeometryOps pin:

| side | worktree | branch @ commit | GeometryOps pin |
|---|---|---|---|
| OLD | `/home/asinghvi17/geo/DGG-hunter` | `claude/perf-hunter` @ `47961ea` | `2825c476` |
| NEW | `/home/asinghvi17/geo/DGG-perf-ladder` | `claude/perf-ladder` @ `7627ee3` | `36c853e0` |

Neither worktree was modified. `regrid_column` is byte-identical in the two
production scripts, so the only variable is the library each project resolves.

**Parameters.** `CopernicusDEMSystem(30)` (3600x3600 px tiles), destination
IGeo7 **level 13**, ancestor **La=6**, so a work unit is again 7^7 = 823 543
cells — the direct analogue of production's La=5 at L12. Conservative,
`Weighted(0.5)`, lazy, `budget = 2^30`. Source is the **full** production
`PartialGrid` over all 26 475 listed tiles at `chunklevel = 0`, not a
handful-of-tiles subset: that is what production does and what makes the
comparison to the 90 m number honest.

**The tile list.** GLO-30 and GLO-90 ship the same 1x1-degree tile lattice; only
the pixel count inside a tile differs. `tileList-glo90.txt` is therefore the
GLO-30 list, reused unchanged.

**Data: 2 tiles are real, no downloads.** Two GLO-30 GeoTIFFs were already on
disk — `N45_00_E010` and `N46_00_E010` — and the columns chosen at 45.5°N and
46.5°N sit inside them, so those two columns decode **real Alpine terrain**.
Verified rather than assumed (`exp:"realcheck"`): column 84178 spans
**61.9–1271.4 m, σ = 220 m**, while the synthetic tropical column spans
700.05–701.09 m, σ = 0.25 m. Nothing was downloaded.

**Load.** The live 90 m production run (`julia -t 26`, PID 3718607) stayed up
throughout, holding ~19.8 cores of its 24-core budget. Load average was **34.5–45.9** across every measurement, recorded
per row in the ndjson. All comparative numbers are `-t 1`; the threading probe
used `-t 8`, the session cap.

## 2. The columns

Level-6 columns are ~1/7 the area of the level-5 production columns, ~0.3° x
0.22° at mid-latitude, so at 30 m a mid-latitude column touches **exactly one**
source tile.

| col | name | centre | extent | tiles | source |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|
| 84178 | `midlat_45N_real` | 10.5°E 45.5°N | 10.33–10.64°E, 45.46–45.68°N | 1 | **real** N45/E010 |
| 84252 | `midlat_46N_real` | 10.5°E 46.5°N | 10.32–10.63°E, 46.45–46.67°N | 1 | **real** N46/E010 |

| 861020 | `polar_85S` | 0.5°E 85.5°S | −2.26–0.83°E, 85.64–85.43°S | 4 | synthetic |

All four are fully land-covered (0 NaN), so these are the **expensive** case; a
real run's coastal and partly-ocean columns are cheaper. The projection in §6 is

## 3. OLD vs NEW at `-t 1`

Warm-up column first (84179, not measured), then 3 reps per column, min wall,
`@timed` for allocation. Every rep is in the ndjson as `exp:"rep"`; rep-to-rep
spread was ≤4 %.

| col | name | OLD core-s | NEW core-s | **speedup** | OLD cells/core-s | NEW cells/core-s | OLD GiB | NEW GiB | Δalloc |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 84178 | midlat 45°N (real) | 32.89 | **11.45** | **2.87x** | 25 039 | 71 925 | 4.309 | 2.845 | −34.0 % |
| 84252 | midlat 46°N (real) | 32.75 | **11.39** | **2.88x** | 25 146 | 72 304 | 4.325 | 2.858 | −33.9 % |
| 389914 | tropical 10°N | 30.49 | **9.16** | **3.33x** | 27 010 | 89 907 | 3.333 | 1.924 | −42.3 % |
| 861020 | polar 85°S | 28.07 | **10.01** | **2.80x** | 29 339 | 82 272 | 3.781 | 2.430 | −35.7 % |
| | **total** | **124.20** | **42.01** | **2.96x** | 26 523 | **78 414** | 15.748 | 10.057 | **−36.1 %** |

**The ladder carries.** Every column beats 2.75x. The mid-latitude pair — the
like-for-like analogue of the 90 m reference column 728, which ran 2.80x — comes
in at **2.87x**. The aggregate 2.96x is pulled up by the tropical column.

**30 m costs about what 90 m did, per column.** NEW column 84178 at 11.45
core-seconds against the 90 m NEW column 728's 10.41: **1.10x the CPU** for the
same 823 543 destination cells. That is the expected shape — a level-13 cell is
1/7 the area of a level-12 one while a 30 m pixel is 1/9 the area of a 90 m one,
so there are 9/7 ≈ 1.29x more source pixels per destination cell, and the
clipper sees proportionally more of them. Allocation rises more than CPU: 2.845
vs 1.874 GiB, **1.52x**, and the −36 % allocation win is a little short of the
90 m −43 %.

**Nothing new dominates.** The brief asked for a flame profile only if the
speedup came in materially smaller. It came in larger, so none was taken.

## 4. Threading at `-t 8`: the c3 handoff engages, except on the polar column

`-t 8` rather than the report's `-t 12`, to respect the session's core cap
against the live run. Two reps each; the `OUTER_PARALLEL = true` control is the
production worker body's shape, which suppresses CR's inner threading and leaves
the wave as the only parallelism — so *default ≈ control* means the default
shape never reached inner threading.

| col | side | default wall | default cores | control wall | control cores | verdict |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 84178 | OLD | 32.62 s | **1.07** | 32.98 s | 1.11 | wave kept, inner threading lost |
| 84178 | **NEW** | **2.53 s** | **5.03** | 11.57 s | 1.24 | **handoff engages** |
| 389914 | OLD | 5.13 s | 6.40 | 30.92 s | 1.14 | already single-chunk |
| 389914 | **NEW** | **1.71 s** | **6.06** | 9.42 s | 1.17 | handoff engages |
| 861020 | OLD | 22.32 s | 1.36 | 22.58 s | 1.37 | wave kept |
| 861020 | **NEW** | 8.12 s | **1.52** | 8.40 s | 1.54 | **wave kept — no handoff** |

Two findings.

1. **On the mid-latitude real-tile column the handoff is worth 12.9x of wall.**
   OLD held 1.07 cores of 8 and finished in 32.62 s; NEW holds **5.03** and
   finishes in **2.53 s**. The control pins the mechanism: OLD's default (1.07
   cores) is indistinguishable from OLD's no-inner-threading control (1.11), so
   OLD's wave was suppressing inner threading and then delivering nothing. This
   is exactly the c3 failure mode, and the column's bounding cap reaching more
   than one listed tile is what triggers it even though the column's *covering*
   is a single tile.
2. **It does not engage on the 4-chunk polar column.** NEW's estimator keeps the
   wave there (default 1.52 cores ≈ control 1.54), and the wave delivers only
   ~1.5 of 8 cores. The pre-existing behaviour is unchanged (OLD 1.36), so this
   is not a regression — but polar columns remain the shape where `-t 8` buys
   almost nothing, and at La=6 there are more of them than at La=5.

**Cores held are real, not burnt.** NEW at `-t 8` runs 64 592 cells/core-s on
84178 and 79 493 on 389914, against 71 925 and 89 907 at `-t 1` — **90 % and
88 % per-core efficiency**. Peak RSS 2.98–3.30 GiB (NEW) vs 3.36 GiB (OLD).

## 6. Projected full GLO-30 land run

**The destination column count was measured, not scaled.** Running the
production covering at La=6 over all 26 475 listed tiles (`exp:"covering"`,
15.7 s at `-t 8`) gives **439 164** level-6 columns — below the 463k the brief
estimated from 66 178 x 7, because the coarser La=5 columns over-cover more.
Total destination cells **3.6167e11**, which is **6.64x** the 90 m run's
5.450e10.

At a 24-core budget, using the measured `-t 1` cells-per-core-second:

| configuration | cells/core-s | core-hours | **wall at 24 cores** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| OLD code | 26 523 | 3 788 | **157.8 h** (6.6 d) |
| **NEW code** | **78 414** | **1 281** | **53.4 h** (2.2 d) |
| NEW, mid-latitude rate only | 72 114 | 1 393 | 58.0 h (2.4 d) |

**~105 core-hours and ~4.3 days of wall clock saved** on a full GLO-30 run.

Using the `-t 1` rate directly for a 24-core `outer`-shape projection is
justified by the 90 m run itself: there the `-t 1` rate (28 291 cells/core-s on
OLD code) and the live production run's observed outer-shape rate (28 614) agree
to 1.5 %, so the outer shape carries essentially no per-core overhead at this
budget. As a cross-check, the same arithmetic reproduces the perf-ladder's own
GLO-90 projection: 5.450e10 / 78 700 / 24 = 8.0 h against its stated ~7.5 h.

Two caveats, both in the conservative direction: all four measured columns are
fully land-covered, whereas a real run's coastal columns pair with less source
and cost less; and c3's shape is not counted as an ETA improvement here, exactly
as in the 90 m report.

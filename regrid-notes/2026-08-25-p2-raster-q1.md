## 1. The fused path

A source whose cells sit on a two-dimensional lattice needs no dual cell object
and no inverse map. Its dual cells are the axis-aligned rectangles between
neighbouring sample sites, and the two bracketing fractions are that
rectangle's own Q1 coordinates, so the basis is `Bilinear` by construction.

Preparation is the two axes, once per source space:

```julia
struct ChartState
    x::_ChartAxis
    y::_ChartAxis
end

samplerstate(space::RegridSpace) = hascellchart(space) ? ChartState(space) : nothing
```

`_ChartAxis` is the one `BilinearPoint` already builds: sample coordinates in
ascending order, the lattice orientation they arrived in, and the axis period.
Nothing duplicates it.

A query is one chart conversion, two bracket searches and at most four products:

```julia
weightsat!(row, s::Sampler{<:RegridSpace,<:AbstractVector,ChartState}, p)
```

The path is chosen by the state a source prepares, not by its type. Any space
answering `hascellchart` takes it — the toy lon/lat space in the tests and
`RasterGrid` in production run the same code. A space returning a state of its
own, such as a prepared `DualCell`, keeps the generic `dualcellat` path.

### The locator

`_bracket(ax, x) -> (i0, i1, w0, w1, status)` sits beside `_locate`, which
clamps and stays for `BilinearPoint`.

- **Lattice indices.** `i0` and `i1` are indices as the space numbers the axis,
  so a descending axis answers its own lookup order. `_ChartAxis` already
  carries `reversed`.
- **Snapping.** A fraction within `1e-12` of an end of its interval is snapped
  to it, at the ends of the axis as well as inside. A destination at a sample
  site therefore emits that site alone with weight exactly one, and roundoff in
  a sphere round trip cannot leave a second entry of weight `1e-17` or push a
  site into the rim.
- **Periodic axes** wrap exactly and are never unmapped: past the last site the
  bracket is that site and the first, across the seam.
- **One-cell axes** have no interval. The single site is bracketed with itself
  at weights `1` and `0`, so the repeat leaves with the zero product and a
  destination never takes two entries with the same index. Such an axis cannot
  exclude a point; the other axis decides. This is the one place the locator
  does not test containment, because `_ChartAxis` carries centres and a
  one-cell axis has no spacing to measure a rim against.
- **Nonperiodic axes** end the Q1 domain at the outermost sample sites. Beyond
  them `x` is not bracketed: within half of the end interval — the rim between
  the outermost sites and the source's boundary — the status is `WeightsRim`,
  and further out `WeightsOutside`. The end interval is the local cell width the
  axis can measure; on an irregular lattice it is that estimate, not an edge
  table.
- **Both axes speak.** A point outside on either axis is `WeightsOutside`; a rim
  only where nothing was outside. The executor reads `ismapped` and neither.

### The support radius

```julia
supportradius(::BarycentricPoint, src_space) =
    _chartradius(samplerstate(src_space), src_space)
```

The larger chart-axis spacing in radians — exactly `BilinearPoint`'s bound,
forwarded to the same arithmetic — on a source the method queries through its
cell chart, and `0.0` on every other source. A chart stencil reaches out to the
bracketing sample sites, up to one source cell beyond the destination's own
extent, so chunk discovery must search that far.

The radius dispatches on prepared state for the same reason `weightsat!` does. A
source that answers dual cells of its own does not take the chart path and must
bound them itself; reading `hascellchart` here instead would demand
`chartspacing` from a space that supplies only `chartcoords`.

## 2. Every difference from `BilinearPoint`

Source is the P0 patch `ToyLonLatSpace(4, 2; lon = (-40, 40), lat = (-20, 20))`
unless stated: longitude centres `-30, -10, 10, 30`, latitude centres
`-10, 10`, both non-periodic, both 20 degrees apart. `BilinearPoint` values are
the P0 pins.

| fixture | `BilinearPoint` | `BarycentricPoint` | plan decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| interior, P0 `ToyLonLatSpace(6,5)` → `(7,4)` | four entries, tensor Q1, row sum one | identical operator, entry for entry | none — raster behavior, same stencil |
| periodic seam, 35° east of the last centre on a 90° lattice | 11/36 and 7/36 on each of two latitudes | identical | raster behavior: "wrap periodic axes exactly" |
| `latrim`, centroid latitude 15 (5 past the last centre) | two entries, 0.75 and 0.25 on the rim line, row sum one | `WeightsRim`, empty row, no entry | decision 8, raster behavior: the Q1 domain ends at the outermost sites; the half-cell strip is a geometric rim |
| `lonrim`, centroid longitude 35 (5 past the last centre) | two entries, 0.35 and 0.65, row sum one | `WeightsRim`, empty row, no entry | decision 8 |
| latitude 19 (just inside the source boundary at 20) | two entries, 0.5 and 0.5 | `WeightsRim`, empty row | decision 8 |
| latitude 21 (just outside the source boundary) | two entries, 0.5 and 0.5 | `WeightsOutside`, empty row | decision 8; rim and outside are told apart at the boundary half a cell past the last site |
| P0 `outside`, latitude 60, `cellat` is `nothing` | two entries, 0.5 and 0.5, row sum one | `WeightsOutside`, empty row | decision 8: "silent clamping is not barycentric interpolation" |
| P0 `west`, chart longitude 190 on a non-periodic axis | two entries, 0.5 and 0.5, row sum one | `WeightsOutside`, empty row | decision 8 |
| a destination exactly at a source sample site | one entry of weight one | identical | acceptance law 3 |
| a one-cell axis | single site, weight one, repeat suppressed | identical | raster behavior: "repeated indices on a one-cell axis are combined before emission" |
| support radius on a source with no cell chart | `ArgumentError` from `_require_chart` | `0.0` | the method answers point queries on sources with no chart |

Every mapped answer is the same as `BilinearPoint`'s, bit for bit on the P0
interior fixture. Every difference is a destination `BilinearPoint` serves by
clamping to a rim line and `BarycentricPoint` leaves to the missing policy.

## 3. What it verifies

`lib/GlobalRegridding/test/test_barycentric.jl`, six new testsets, 52 new
assertions:

- the interior operator equal to `BilinearPoint`'s under `==` on dense
  matrices, four entries per row, rows summing to one, `a + bx + cy + dxy`
  reproduced, and zero bytes allocated by a warm `weightsat!`;
- the periodic seam's 11/36 and 7/36;
- both rim fixtures unmapped with an empty row while the clamped pins still
  hold, the rim/outside boundary pinned on both sides of the source's edge, and
  both outside fixtures `WeightsOutside`;
- the identity operator on a destination lattice sitting on the source's sites,
  on a non-periodic source and a periodic one, with one entry per row;
- a `RasterGrid` with a descending latitude lookup matching sites with the
  ascending one, Q1 reproduced on an interior destination over it, and a
  one-cell latitude axis giving two entries per row and the identity at its own
  sites;
- the support radius equal to `BilinearPoint`'s and `0.0` on a chartless
  source; the pair-assembled operator equal to the eager one; eager equal to
  chunked and to a differently chunked source; and a destination whose stencil
  reaches a source chunk its own cell corners lie outside, which the plan's
  dependency relation names.

One existing assertion set moved: "the sampler prepares a source space once"
now uses a wrapper with no cell chart, because the toy lon/lat space it used
has one and now prepares a `ChartState`. Its assertion count is unchanged.

## 4. What follows from it

- The chunk-pair `buildweights!` still runs one point query per
  `(destination tile, source chunk)` pair. The fused query makes each of those
  queries cheap; it does not remove the repetition. That is P3's number, and the
  support radius above is what keeps the pre-P3 pair route free of discovery
  false negatives.
- `WeightsRim` now has a producer. A destination in the half-cell strip of a
  raster is missing rather than clamped, which is a visible behaviour change for
  any caller who moves from `BilinearPoint` to `BarycentricPoint` over a
  regional raster.
- A space claiming `hascellchart` must supply all five chart hooks the moment it
  is asked for a support radius through the chart path. A space that supplies
  only `chartcoords`, to write dual cells of its own, must return its own
  `samplerstate` — as it must anyway to answer `dualcellat`.

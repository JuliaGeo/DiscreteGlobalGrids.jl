# F3 — performance fixup (P2's accepted proposals)

`regrid-notes/perf-P2.md`'s Q3 (chunk-extent caps), Q1 (the recomputed cell
geometry, in the half `lib` owns), Q2 (threading the lazy path's block builds),
the pole-discovery assessment, and the `Rasters` weakdep F2 deferred. Files
touched, all under `lib/GlobalRegridding`:
`src/{rastergrid,conservative,spaces,plans,lazy}.jl`,
`ext/GlobalRegriddingRastersExt.jl` (new), `Project.toml`,
`test/{toyspaces,test_conservative,test_executor,test_integration,test_lazy}.jl`.
The main package's `src/` was not touched; its `Manifest.toml` gained four lines
of extension wiring and nothing else.

## How the numbers were taken

| | |
|---|---|
| environment | Julia 1.12.6, macOS (Darwin 25.5.0, Apple silicon, 8 CPU threads), worktree `../DGG-regrid` at `claude/regrid`, CR `agent/use-geometryops-main-cache`, GeometryOps git-tree `77dcc16f` |
| lazy case | T10's acceptance setup verbatim: `DEMTiles` 3600 × 1800 `Float32` in 300² tiles → `IGeo7System()` at level 7, 8 235 432 cells / 3432 chunks. South-pole chunk 3374 (12 connected tiles), mid-latitude chunk 245 (2) |
| eager case | P1's mid size: `RasterGrid` 3600 × 1800 chunked 512² → 360 × 180, whole-domain block, 7 120 800 nonzeros |
| method | fresh `julia --project=... --threads=N` processes; every before/after is an A/B **inside one process**, switching methods with `Core.eval` at top level |
| identity | `hash(A[POLARCELLS])` on the lazy side, `hash((colptr, rowval, nzval, denom))` on the eager side |

**A world-age trap, recorded because it silently voided the first three
measurements.** `Core.eval` inside a top-level `for` loop is invisible to the
calls in that same loop — the loop is one thunk and its world age is fixed
before the redefinition. Every A/B here therefore calls the switched function
through `Base.invokelatest`. Before that was noticed the same script reported
"no change" for a change that is worth 23 %. F1's log says "all switching is
top-level" for exactly this reason; a top-level *loop* is not top level enough.

## Item 1 — the chunk-extent cap, restored to sixteen samples

`_rectcap` is called in two regimes and F2's four-corner `_sampledcap` is right
for only one of them. `_chunkcap` is the chunk-extent form — the sixteen-sample
`_boxcap` against the same `_widecap` alternative — and `chunktree(::RasterGrid)`
is its one caller. `_rectcap` keeps the corner form for node extents, where it
is called once per visited node pair.

| | P1 | P2 (F2's caps) | F3 | truth |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| tile-chunked destination (60 × 60) | 205 | **210** | **205** | 78 |
| band-chunked destination (360 × 20) | 218 | 150 | **150** | 96 |
| destination tile cap | — | 38.50° | **37.7584°** | |
| source chunk cap | — | 31.36° | **30.8396°** | |

P2's prediction to the pair and to the second decimal. At the acceptance scale
(3432 destination chunks against 72 source tiles) total discovered pairs go
**9052 → 8930**, −1.3 %: a 30°-wide source tile is compact enough that the corner
cap was already close, which is why P1's 60°-wide destination tiles are where
the difference shows.

**The per-node cost did not move**, which is the thing to prove because F1
measured a 19 % regression from a call in the wrong place:

- `_rectcap` is **bit-identical** to the pre-refactor construction — over 300
  random index rectangles of the 3600 × 1800 raster, `0` differ in centre or
  radius (`===` per component).
- Whole-domain eager build at 1/9 scale, interleaved medians of four in one
  process: **0.6696 s** refactored against **0.6930 s** original — the shared
  tail moving into `_tighterof` costs nothing.
- P1's mid-size eager build, fresh processes: **7.224 s @8t** (P2: 7.15 s) and
  **37.955 s @1t** (P2: 37.83 s), 34.15 / 33.23 GiB, nnz **7 120 800**, digest
  **17190587724216142268** — P2's digest exactly, at both thread counts.

## Item 2 — one destination tile's cell geometry, synthesized once

### What the recompute actually is, and which half `lib` owns

P2 attributes 42.6 % of a cold build to `cell_boundary`, reached two ways: the
DGGS cursor's `child_indices_extents` → `cell_cap`, and `Trees.getcell` → the
destination polygon the clip is handed. The first is `src/fallbacks/{cursor,
caps}.jl` in the main package and is out of scope by instruction. The second is
`lib`'s consumption pattern and is what this item removes.

Measured on the acceptance case, not inferred: `ConservativeRegridding`'s
`_run_and_store!` calls `Trees.getcell(dst_tree, i2)` once per **candidate
pair**, and the south-pole tile's twelve pairs have **269 600** candidate pairs
over **2401** destination cells. One pair alone (source tile 61) has 48 897
candidates over 538 distinct destination cells — 91 syntheses per cell. One
`getcell` of an IGEO7 level-7 cell is 1.1 µs; one pass over the whole tile is
**2.59 ms**, 1.63 MiB allocated, **0.605 MiB** resident.

### The cache and why its scoping is correct

`TileCells(space, inds)` is a `RegridSpace` decorator, and `CachedCellTree` is
the tree it hands a weight builder. Four properties, in the order they matter:

1. **Scope is one destination tile's production, not the plan and not a
   build.** `_readdestination!` constructs one `TileCells` per tile inside the
   tile loop and passes it to that tile's `blockfor` calls; it is unreachable
   once the tile is written and dies with it. Peak is one tile's store, because
   the tile loop is sequential. Memory is `O(cells in the tile)` — 0.605 MiB for
   2401 IGEO7 cells — and tiles over 65 536 cells decline the store and behave
   exactly as before.

   *This is a deviation from the brief's "per build", taken on a measurement:
   the store costs one pass, and a tile has 2–12 builds, so per-build scoping
   pays 12 × 2.59 ms where per-tile pays 2.59 ms once. The per-build form is
   still what `blockfor(plan, key, dinds)` does when no store is handed to it,
   so the default is the conservative one and the sharing is opt-in at the one
   call site that owns a whole tile.*

2. **Nothing is materialized until something is built.** The store is empty at
   construction and is filled on the first `subtree` over the tile's own index
   set — the tree the conservative descent clips against. A warm read builds
   nothing, so it synthesizes nothing: **27.81 ms / 4.22 MiB @8t and 28.29 ms /
   4.21 MiB @1t**, against P2's 27.86 ms / 4.42 MiB, with `builds` still 12
   afterwards. A method that never asks for a subtree — the toy diagonal method,
   the radius method — never triggers it either, which is why a space that
   cannot answer `getcell` still works.

3. **`getcell` stays lazy for everyone else.** `getcell(::TileCells, i)`
   forwards to the space, unconditionally. The store is a property of the
   *tree*, so nothing that holds the space sees materialization and the
   `O(1)`-space construction is untouched. The eager `DirectPlan` path
   (`api.jl`) is not routed through `TileCells` at all — F2's decision not to
   materialize 800 000 polygons for a whole-domain build stands.

4. **Thread safety is by immutability, not by locking the hot path.** The fill
   happens once, under a `ReentrantLock`, before the tree exists; after that the
   `Vector` is read-only and is shared by every concurrent build of the tile and
   by every assembly task inside them. `CachedCellTree` has no mutable field.
   The lock is taken once per build (12 times per polar read), never per cell.
   The untyped field is read through a function barrier
   (`_cachedtree(tree, map, cells::Vector{P})`), so the clip loop sees a concrete
   element type — one dynamic dispatch per build, in the same place CR already
   pays one for `task_local_operator`. A space whose cells are not one concrete
   type declines the store rather than putting a dispatch in the clip loop.

### What it bought

Cold read of one destination chunk, fresh process, median of five:

| | 1 thread | 8 threads |
|---|---:|---:|
| south pole (12 pairs), before | 1.0373 s | 0.6226 s |
| south pole, with the store | **0.7947 s (−23 %)** | 0.5846 s (−6 %) |
| mid-latitude (2 pairs), before | 0.0563 s | 0.0400 s |
| mid-latitude, with the store | **0.0443 s (−21 %)** | 0.0405 s |

The 8-thread column is small because the build was already threaded there: the
work removed is spread over the same eight threads that were doing it. The
1-thread column is the honest measure of the work removed, and −23 % is a little
over half of the 42.6 % P2 attributed to `cell_boundary` — the rest is the
cursor's `cell_cap`, which is the main package's.

`hash(A[POLARCELLS])` is **identical** in every configuration and at both thread
counts (`6078816503359009746` for the pole, `4025814376466885066` for the
mid-latitude chunk).

## Item 3 — a wave of concurrent block builds

`_readdestination!` now builds a tile's pairs a **wave** at a time and applies
the wave in chunk order. `_fillwave!` spawns one task per pair and fetches in
order; `_wavesize` decides how wide.

**The accumulation order does not change**, so neither does any `Float64`: the
apply loop still walks `srcchunks` in ascending order, one wave after another.
Only the *building* is concurrent, and building reads nothing — the source is
read in the apply loop, once per chunk per group, exactly as before. The
acceptance file's "twelve reads, all in the southern tile row" and the lib
suite's L2 read-count assertions hold unchanged.

**The wave is bounded by the weight budget, not by the thread count alone.** Its
blocks are held until it is applied, so an unbounded wave would put a whole
fan-in's weights in memory at a budget that asks for one block. The bound uses
what is known before a build — a block's column pointers and its reference
vector:

| `budget` | `weightbudget` | wave |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2^20 (the acceptance file's streaming case) | 262 144 | **1** |
| 2^24 | 4 194 304 | 5 |
| 2^30 (default) | 268 435 456 | 8 = `nthreads` |

At one thread, or for a tile with one connected chunk, `_wavesize` is 1 and the
loop is the pre-F3 loop.

**Nesting: measured, both ways, and the nested form kept.** The alternative was
to bind the inner descent/clip threading off inside a wave (a `ScopedValue`
around `_intersectionareas`), on the theory that two levels oversubscribe.
Medians of five, 8 threads, cold read:

| | wave + inner serial | wave + inner threaded (kept) | no wave |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| south pole, 12 pairs | 0.281 s | **0.260 s** | 0.617 s |
| mid-latitude, 2 pairs | 0.047 s | **0.038 s** | 0.039 s |

Nested wins on both shapes, and the two-pair tile says why: a wave narrower than
the session's threads leaves them idle, and forcing the inner descent serial
there is a **20 % loss** against doing nothing at all. The waves are uneven in
size too, so the threads a finished pair leaves go to the ones still running.
The `ScopedValue` switch was measured and then deleted rather than left in.

### What it bought

Cold read, fresh process, median of five, the shipped state against the state at
`6f40cbe`:

| | 1 thread | 8 threads | speedup @8t |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| south pole, before | 1.0373 s | 0.6226 s | 1.67× |
| south pole, after | 0.7912 s | **0.2457 s** | **3.22×** |
| mid-latitude, before | 0.0563 s | 0.0400 s | 1.41× |
| mid-latitude, after | 0.0446 s | **0.0348 s** | 1.28× |

The polar cold read is **2.53× faster** than it was at eight threads, and the
lazy path's own threading speedup goes from P2's **1.6× to 3.22×**. Items 2 and
3 are separable in that table: the store is what moves the 1-thread column, the
wave is what moves the 8-thread one.

One behaviour changes with it, and it is worth knowing: a build that throws
inside a wave of more than one pair now surfaces through `fetch` as a
`TaskFailedException` wrapping the original — the manifold-mismatch
`ArgumentError`, say. The message is still in the printed error and still
matches a string-based `@test_throws`, but a test matching the exception *type*
from a lazy read would not. A wave of one calls `blockfor` directly and is
unchanged.

### One thing the wave broke, and it was the test doubles

`T7CountingMethod`, `CountingMethod` and `T6CountingMethod` count builds with
`method.builds += 1`, which is a read-modify-write that two tasks can interleave
and lose. Concurrent builds made the LRU and `Spilled` testsets flaky —
`builds == 2 * builds` failing 15 against 14, `spilledfiles == built` failing 8
against 7, both from a *lost* count rather than an extra build. The counters now
go through `countbuild!`, one lock in `toyspaces.jl`; no assertion was weakened.
Four consecutive suite runs at `-t8` are stable.

## Item 4 — the pole's four empty tiles cannot be discovered away

Re-measured after item 1: the south-pole chunk still discovers **12** source
tiles and reads 12, of which 4 produce no nonzeros. **No cap construction can do
better, and this is a proof rather than a failed attempt.**

The destination chunk's cap has radius **2.707°** and *contains the south pole*
(its centre is 1.855° from it) — it must, because the chunk's cells surround the
pole. Every one of the twelve southernmost source tiles spans rows 1501:1800,
whose southern edge is latitude −90 exactly, so **the pole is a point of every
tile** and every cap that covers a tile contains it (verified: `_contains(cap,
pole)` is `true` for all twelve, each of radius 16.25°). Two caps that share a
point intersect, so `d(centres) ≤ r₁ + r₂` holds for all twelve by construction
— measured 12.84 … 16.49 against 18.95.

Tightening a polar cap therefore cannot drop a tile: the pole is in the
intersection whatever the radii. Dropping the four would need a different extent
*vocabulary* — P1's option (a), a lon/lat box overlap test a `RasterGrid` can
answer exactly — which is a design change and not a fixup. Reported and stopped,
as instructed. T10's "12 tiles read" assertion is still the right assertion
against the current vocabulary; P2's Q4 rewording is not needed and would weaken
a mutant killer.

## Item 5 — the `Rasters` extension

`ext/GlobalRegriddingRastersExt.jl` defines the one method F2 specified:

```julia
GlobalRegridding.sourcemissingval(A::Rasters.AbstractRaster) =
    _sentinel(Rasters.missingval(A))
```

`Rasters.missingval(::AbstractRaster)` is `A.missingval`, a **field**, which is
why the package's metadata route answers `nothing` for every raster (F2 verified
the GDAL path carries only `"filepath"`). Both of Rasters' "no sentinel"
spellings map to `nothing`: `nothing` itself, and `missing` for an array whose
element type already carries `Missing`. That is not just a convention — it is
the faster and the equivalent reading, since `isvalidvalue` already rejects
`missing` on its own, `isequal(x, missing)` is `false` for every non-missing
`x`, and `missingval === nothing` is the branch that compiles away and lets
`anyinvalid` keep its element-type shortcut. Every other value passes through,
including a `NaN` sentinel and an integer one.

**F2's resolution blocker did not materialize.** `Rasters 0.15.0` resolves
against all three pinned repo revs. The wiring is `[weakdeps]`, `[extensions]`,
`compat Rasters = "0.15"`, and `Rasters` in `[extras]`/`[targets] test` — so the
extension is *loaded* by the suite that tests it, which is the only arrangement
worth having. `lib/GlobalRegridding/Manifest.toml` gained the self-entry's
weakdeps/extensions block and nothing else; the three `repo-rev` lines are
untouched. The workspace `Manifest.toml` gained exactly four lines under
`[[deps.GlobalRegridding]]` (`weakdeps` + the `extensions` block), added by
`Pkg.resolve()` run from `test/` — the safe direction T10 and F2 both name; the
GeometryOps revs are byte-identical afterwards, checked by diff. No throwaway
environment was needed.

`Pkg.test` reports `✓ GlobalRegridding → GlobalRegriddingRastersExt` and the six
assertions in `test_executor.jl` cover the sentinel, the `NaN` sentinel, both
"no sentinel" spellings, the field-beats-metadata case, and the end-to-end claim
that a raster of sentinels regrids to what the same field with `NaN` regrids to
with no keyword passed.

One thing a reader should know: `docs/Project.toml` does **not** list
`GlobalRegridding`, so `import GlobalRegridding` fails under `--project=docs`.
No tutorial imports it any more, so nothing is broken, but the extension cannot
fire in the docs environment until that dep is added. Not touched here.

## New names (all unexported, reachable as `GlobalRegridding.x`)

- **`TileCells`** — the destination decorator. The seam a space that wants its
  own materialization strategy would specialize, and the one name here worth
  considering for export alongside `subtree`.
- **`CachedCellTree`** — the tree it hands a builder; every
  `SpatialTreeInterface` method forwards to the wrapped tree untouched.
- `blockfor` and `buildblock` gained a four-argument form taking the destination
  space to build against; the old arities are unchanged and still work.
- Private: `_chunkcap`, `_tighterof` (`rastergrid.jl`); `_tilecells!`,
  `_synthesizecells`, `_cachedtree`, `_cachedposition`, `_TILE_CELL_CACHE_MAX`
  (`conservative.jl`); `_wavesize`, `_fillwave!` (`lazy.jl`).
- Test-side: `countbuild!` and `TOY_COUNT_LOCK` in `toyspaces.jl`, which every
  counting double now goes through.

Still open from F2/T8/T10: `chunkat`, `sourcemissingval` (now with a shipped
extension behind it, which strengthens the case), `knownempty`, `isvalidvalue`,
`residency`, `LazyStats`, `weightbudget`, `databudget`, `spilledfiles`,
`usesreference`, and T9's question about re-exporting the method and policy
names from `DiscreteGlobalGrids`.

## What is left on the table

1. **The DGGS cursor's `cell_cap`** — the other half of P2's 42.6 %, out of
   scope by instruction. It is `points_cap ∘ cell_boundary` per leaf visit in
   `src/fallbacks/{cursor,caps}.jl`, and the polar chunk's subtree has one cell
   per leaf, so nothing amortizes it. `lib` cannot reach it: a leaf's indices are

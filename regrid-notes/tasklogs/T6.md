# T6 — eager integration + conservation laws

`test/test_integration.jl` (33 assertions) plus seam fixes in `src/rastergrid.jl`,
`src/executor.jl`, `src/api.jl`, and one testset updated in
`test/test_executor.jl`. Full suite green: **260 pass, 1 broken** (T3's
GeometryOps non-convex-clip case), with T7's `Lazy path` at 53 passing.

The eager path now runs end to end on the real components: a DimensionalData
raster in, `RasterGrid` derived from it, `Conservative`/`NearestCell`/
`BilinearPoint` weights, `DirectPlan`, executor, N-D shaping.

## Seams closed

1. **`RasterGrid`'s chart accessor** (`rastergrid.jl`, T2 seam 1). `chartaxes`,
   `chartcoords`, `chartposition`, `chartperiod`, `chartspacing` — the methods
   precede the generics they extend, which `interpolation.jl` declares later;
   nothing runs at load time, so the order costs only reading comfort.
   - `chartperiod` reports `360.0` **only** when the X edges span the chart's
     whole period. A regional raster on a longitude chart does not close, and a
     reported period would wrap its stencils onto the far edge of the domain.
   - `chartcoords` puts a periodic native X on the branch the edge vector is
     written on, the same reduction `cellat` does, so a `0:360` raster answers
     350 rather than −10.
   - `chartspacing` converts the largest edge step through a **new hook**,
     `chartarcs(transform, dx, dy) -> (Δx, Δy)` in radians of arc — the third of
     the `chartinverse`/`chartlimits` family, with a `LonLatToSphere` method and
     an erroring default naming itself. A projected chart defines it or forgoes
     `BilinearPoint`'s support radius.
   - `hascellchart(::RasterGrid)` is now `inverse !== nothing` (see T2.md).
2. **`subtree(::RasterGrid, inds)`** (`rastergrid.jl`, T2 seam 2). `_indexrect`
   recognizes an index set that enumerates a lattice box in the space's own
   position order — every chunk, and `1:ncells` — and returns the `O(1)`
   `RasterCellTree` for it; anything else falls back to the flat tree. It
   recovers the rectangle from the positions alone, so a chunk that is a
   `Vector{Int}` (not spanning the fastest dimension) keeps the cheap tree too.
   The eager whole-domain path is unchanged by this; **T7/T8 are what it is
   for** — without it every chunk pair paid one polygon per cell for its BVH.
3. **`from === nothing`** (`api.jl`). `_sourcespace` dispatches: a
   `DD.AbstractDimArray` builds `RasterGrid(data)`, anything else throws an
   `ArgumentError` saying the array carries no coordinates and naming both ways
   out. Previously a plain `Array` reached `RasterGrid` and produced a
   `MethodError`. The kwarg itself is kept — review it at T9, when DGG can
   resolve a source from `to`'s package.

## Semantics change, and the law that forced it

`finalize!`'s `Weighted` branch now divides by `cover` for **every** block, not
only for blocks that declared a denominator.

T5's carve-out ("a method's denominator is its declaration of what full coverage
weighs, and only a block that declares one is normalized") contradicted three
other documents — the design's "coverage-normalized mean", `Weighted`'s own
frozen docstring in `methods.jl` ("divided by the accumulated weight of the
source cells that were **not** missing"), and T4's log — and it broke acceptance
law 3 for the shipped point methods. Measured before the fix: a constant-5 source
raster with **one** NaN cell, regridded with `BilinearPoint()` and `Weighted()`
onto a half-cell-offset destination, returned `3.75` at the four destinations
whose stencil touched the NaN. A point method's support reaches past the cells a
destination overlaps, so that is exactly "NaN padding outside the destination's
footprint changed a destination value".

Clean supports are untouched — `cover` is the row sum, which is `1` for both
point methods — so `NearestCell`/`BilinearPoint` results are bit-identical
wherever no source was missing, and `Conservative` is unaffected throughout.
What changed is that a truncated support is renormalized over what survived
rather than returned scaled down, and `threshold` (not the block) decides whether
it is kept at all: `Weighted(1)` is "whole supports only". `finalize!`'s
`hasdenom` argument is now inert — kept because T7 calls both forms; a later wave
may prune it. `test_executor.jl`'s "block without a denominator" testset was
updated, and T5.md carries the corrective line.

**Not changed, and worth a decision:** `Weighted`'s threshold is measured against
the *block's denominator*, i.e. the destination's covered area, not the
destination cell's own area. A destination cell that a regional source overlaps
by 1% therefore has `cover == total` and is kept, not blanked. That is what the
frozen `Weighted` docstring says and what keeps finalize free of polygon
construction (`cellarea` exists for the alternative), but it means "fraction
covered by the source at all" is not expressible through `missingpolicy` today.
T9/T10 should confirm this is the wanted behaviour at a DGGS boundary.

## The laws, and where they live

All in `test/test_integration.jl`, none using `toyspaces.jl`.

| testset | law | what it kills |
|---|---|---|
| `conservation` | 3 | `Extensive` global integral vs `Σ f·area`, and the destination-side dual (`Weighted` × destination areas). Column sums must be source cell areas; a transposed or rescaled operator fails. |
| `NaN padding invariance` | 3 | Padding a source from ±60° to the whole sphere with NaN leaves inner destinations identical; all-NaN destinations blank; the bilinear partial-stencil case above. |
| `orientation` | 4 | An asymmetric field on a reverse-latitude raster **and** on a `(Y, X)`-stored one give identical destination values. Rings and flattening both derive from `dims(A)`, so they cannot disagree. |
| `N-D in one plan` | 2 | 12 monthly fields, one call, `builds == 1` through a counting method; month dim survives; every slice equals its own 2-D regrid. |
| `cross-method agreement` | — | `NearestCell` (0.065) and `BilinearPoint` (0.038) within 0.15 of `Conservative` on a smooth field varying by ~3. |
| `bilinear across the longitude seam` | — | A destination centred exactly on ±180 averages sin(175°) with sin(−175°) to 0; without the periodicity `chartperiod` reports it returns ±0.087. A regional raster reports no period. |
| `raster subtrees` | — | Every chunk's `subtree` is a `RasterCellTree`, an arbitrary index list a `RasterFlatTree`. |

No `@test_broken` was needed: every destination here is a four-corner graticule
quad and therefore convex, so the Sutherland–Hodgman non-convex-clip defect
(isolated in `test_conservative.jl`) does not bite. Conservation holds to
`rtol = 1e-10`; measured relative error is ~1.4e-15.

## For T8 / T9 / T10

- `subtree(::RasterGrid, inds)` is the whole reason a chunked raster plan is
  cheap. A DGGS space (T9) owes the same method — `_indexrect` is the shape to
  copy: recognize your own canonical index runs, fall back otherwise.
- A DGGS space that wants `BilinearPoint` must implement all five chart methods
  **and** report a `chartperiod` that is honest about closing; see §7 question 2
  of the design, still open.
- `regrid` of a `DimArray` returns dims `(Dim{:Cell}(1:ndst), others...)`. A
  `RasterGrid` **destination** therefore comes back flattened, not 2-D. T9 owns
  replacing the cell axis; a raster→raster tutorial (T10) needs
  `reshape(out, rastersize(dst))` until then.
- `chartarcs` and `_indexrect` are new unexported names in `rastergrid.jl`, on
  top of T2's list.
